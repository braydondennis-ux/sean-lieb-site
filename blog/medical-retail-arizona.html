Medical Retail: The Tenant Quietly Reshaping Arizona Strip Centers

By Sean Lieb · Aug 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Walk a Valley strip center that a decade ago was all soft goods and cell-phone stores, and you'll notice who's signing leases now: urgent care, dental, dermatology, physical therapy, dialysis, med spas and boutique fitness. Health and wellness has quietly become one of the most active forces backfilling Arizona retail — and for landlords, it's some of the best tenancy you can land.

"Medical retail" — healthcare delivered in a convenient, retail-style storefront instead of a hospital campus — has been one of the sector's most durable growth stories. In a market like Greater Phoenix, the tailwinds are especially strong.

Why medical tenants are so attractive

From a landlord's seat, a good medical tenant checks nearly every box:

  • E-commerce proof. You can't get a filling, an X-ray or physical therapy shipped to your door. This is demand that has to happen in person.
  • Needs-based and recurring. Patients come back — often on a schedule — which drives steady, predictable foot traffic that benefits the whole center.
  • Strong credit and long leases. Many operators are backed by regional or national groups and sign longer terms, often with meaningful tenant investment in the buildout.
  • Sticky by design. Once a practice sinks six or seven figures into exam rooms, plumbing and equipment, it isn't moving on a whim. That capital investment translates into renewals.
A tenant that pours real money into the space and brings patients back weekly is exactly the anchor a small-shop rent roll wants.

Why Arizona amplifies the trend

Two Valley-specific dynamics make medical retail especially active here:

  • Population & demographics. Metro Phoenix keeps adding residents, including a sizable share of retirees and active-adult households — a demographic that consumes a lot of healthcare, close to home.
  • Growth corridors need services. As master-planned communities fill in on the edges, healthcare follows the rooftops. New trade areas create fresh demand for urgent care, dental and wellness right where people live.

What landlords should weigh

Medical tenants are excellent, but they come with specifics. Plumbing, electrical, HVAC and ADA requirements are heavier than a typical shop; parking ratios matter (patients plus staff); and some uses need particular zoning or landlord consent. The upside — strong credit, long term, durable traffic — usually more than justifies the accommodation, but it's worth underwriting the buildout and use up front.

What operators should know

For a practice choosing a site, the retail fundamentals still rule: visibility, easy in-and-out access, co-tenancy that shares your patient base, and a trade area with the right demographics. The best medical corners get leased quietly through relationships before they ever hit a sign — which is exactly where good representation earns its keep.

The takeaway

Medical retail isn't flashy, and that's the point. It's durable, needs-based demand with strong credit and long leases — the kind of tenancy that stabilizes a center for years. In a growth market like Arizona with an aging, expanding population, expect health and wellness to keep taking space in the Valley's strip centers.
