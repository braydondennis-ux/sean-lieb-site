Why Grocery-Anchored Centers Anchor Phoenix Retail

By Sean Lieb · Aug 11, 2026 · 5 min read

When investors ask me where the safest yield in Arizona retail lives, my answer rarely changes: the well-located, grocery-anchored center. Cycle after cycle, they prove to be the most durable income in the sector — and Greater Phoenix keeps making the case.

The appeal isn't complicated. A grocery store is a needs-based tenant that draws customers in good times and bad, several trips a week, rain or shine. That foot traffic doesn't just benefit the anchor — it feeds every shop, service and restaurant in the center. Underwrite the anchor correctly and the small-shop income tends to take care of itself.

Traffic that doesn't blink

Grocery is one of the few retail categories that has largely shrugged off e-commerce. People still want to pick their own produce and grab dinner on the way home, and even online grocery often routes through a physical store. That translates into consistent trips — the lifeblood of the inline tenants who pay a center's higher-margin rents.

The anchor sells groceries. What it really produces is repeat foot traffic — and that's what the whole center is buying.

Why Phoenix amplifies the thesis

Two Valley-specific dynamics make grocery-anchored centers especially strong here:

  • Rooftops keep coming. Metro Phoenix's steady population growth means new grocery trade areas are still being created on the edges — and grocers compete hard for the best corners.
  • Supply stays tight. Very little new multi-tenant retail is being built, so a stabilized grocery center with a strong anchor is a scarce, hard-to-replace asset.

Scarcity plus durable demand is exactly the setup that keeps quality centers well-occupied and rents climbing.

What to actually underwrite

Not every grocery-anchored deal is equal. Before I get a client comfortable, I want to see:

  • Anchor sales performance. Strong per-store sales signal a committed anchor that will renew — the single most important variable.
  • Lease term and options. How much anchor term remains, and how are renewals priced?
  • Small-shop health. A resilient mix of necessity and service tenants, not a roster dependent on one category.
  • The real trade area. Demographics and competition that support the whole center, not just the grocer.

The takeaway

Grocery-anchored retail isn't glamorous, and that's precisely the point. It's the boring, dependable engine of a retail portfolio — durable traffic, needs-based demand and scarcity value, all in one asset. In a growth market like Phoenix with a tight supply of quality space, that combination is tough to beat.
